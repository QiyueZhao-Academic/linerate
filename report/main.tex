\title{The Crossover Payload of an Encrypted UDP Data Plane}

\author{Qiyue Zhao}

\begin{abstract}
We show that the per-packet cost of an encrypted UDP data plane splits into a
fixed term $a$ and a per-byte term $b$ whose ratio, the crossover payload
$s^{*}=a/b$, is the one quantity that transfers between machines when neither
term alone does. Firstly, we fit that model over \eOneUnits\ configurations
covering four cipher implementations, the hardware crypto instructions present
and masked, and the whole payload range an MTU allows. Secondly, we measure what
the system-call boundary contributes to $a$ directly, from a submission path
that issues no system calls, instead of extrapolating it from a batching curve.
Moreover, we apply the same fixed-versus-marginal split at two further
boundaries: the isolation layer around a virtual network function, and the bus
to a GPU. Finally, every control states its prediction before it runs, and every
number in this text is generated from the dataset rather than typed into it. On
the host measured here \bestCipher\ gives $a=\bestA$ cycles and $b=\bestB$
cycles per payload byte, so $s^{*}=\bestSStar$ bytes against a largest carriable
payload of \protoMaxPayload\ bytes, and the fixed cost therefore dominates at
every size this data plane can send. Against OpenSSL, the implementation written
for this study holds the fixed term to a factor of \simdVsOpensslA\ at
\simdVsOpensslB\ times the per-byte cost, and \ctlAEADOnlyFixed\ of its
\ctlAEADFixed\ cycles of fixed cost are the AEAD's own.
\end{abstract}
